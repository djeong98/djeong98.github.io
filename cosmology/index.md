This is not a lecture course with AI added to it. The premise is that AI systems now produce cosmology derivations, code, and literature summaries of high apparent quality, and that the scarce skill is no longer generating such material but judging whether it is correct.

The course trains one loop, repeatedly:

> Think → Ask AI → Inspect → Challenge → Verify → Revise → Synthesize → Ask the next question.

In the first six weeks you apply it to problems whose answers are known. In the project that runs through the second half of the term, you apply it to a question nobody has answered.

Class time is not spent on lectures. You read, you solve eight problems a week, and in each meeting four of you work them at the blackboard while the other four take them apart. Use AI freely while you prepare; the blackboard is where it is established whether the work is yours.

## One thing to settle at the outset

Cosmology has a short list of very well measured numbers and it is easy to mistake them for the subject. Consider what the precision is hiding.

We know the dark matter density to a per cent and we do not know what dark matter is. We know the dark energy density better than that and have no account of it whatsoever. We knew the baryon density to a per cent for twenty years while being unable to say where most of the baryons were, and that accounting closed only recently. And "the CMB is a blackbody" is a statement about a bound, measured in 1990, not improved since, and sitting three orders of magnitude above the distortion that $\Lambda$CDM predicts and nobody has been funded to look for.

Each of these numbers is a statement about what the universe contains or how it began, and the reason to know it to three digits is that the third digit distinguishes between things that could be true. A parameter carried without that content is not knowledge of anything, and it is the part of the work an AI already does better than you.
