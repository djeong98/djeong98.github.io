ASTRO/PHYS 545 · Week 1

Cosmology, and
how to tell whether
a claim is right.

Donghui Jeong · Department of Astronomy & Astrophysics · Penn State

Start here

Cosmology has a short list of
very well measured numbers.

It is easy to mistake them for the subject. Consider what the precision is hiding.

1%We know the dark matter density this well.
We do not know what dark matter is.
betterWe know the dark energy density better than that.
We have no account of it whatsoever.
20 yrWe knew the baryon density to a per cent for twenty years
while unable to say where most of the baryons were.
1990"The CMB is a blackbody" is a bound measured in 1990,
never improved, sitting three orders of magnitude above
the distortion $\Lambda$CDM predicts.

Every one of these is a statement about what the universe contains or how it began. The third digit matters because it separates things that could be true.

The premise

AI now produces derivations, code and
literature summaries of high apparent quality.

So the scarce skill is no longer generating that material.
It is judging whether it is correct.

Carrying a parameter without its physical content is exactly the part of the work an AI already does better than you.

What we practise

One loop, every week,
for fifteen weeks.

Think → Ask AI → Inspect → Challenge → Verify → Revise → Synthesize → Ask the next question.

Weeks 2–6: on problems whose answers are known.
Second half of the term: on a question nobody has answered.

Consequence

Which of the four problems you get is drawn at the start of class. So you prepare all four.

Two roles, every class

Presenter · 4 of you

Reproduce the argument on the board by hand. State where each assumption enters. Mark what you could not resolve.

Challenger · 4 of you

Find the weakest step and press it. A question that only asks for repetition does not count.

Roles swap between Tuesday and Thursday. Everyone has a job in every class. With eight of us there is no back row.

Say this out loud once

Getting stuck at the board is course material, not failure.

Thirty seconds on your own. Then the floor opens. Then I step in. Where the argument broke gets written down, and that is where your repair starts.

Errors made and worked through at the board are not penalised. Reciting a memorised solution is.

The week

Solved once, digested twice.

Mon noonSolutions due, all eight problems

The final version shows what changed and why you had not caught it. That history is the graded part. A solution that was wrong and correctly diagnosed scores above one that was right and unexamined.

Written without AI

Two lines, one at each end
of every submission.

Opening

The point in your solutions you were least able to verify.

Closing

Where AI was wrong, incomplete, or confidently vague, and how you found out. If it was not, say so.

These two lines are the only written record this course keeps of how you work. I read the eight opening lines before every Tuesday.

Three times · weeks 2, 4, 6

Problem sets are the things
that do not fit on a blackboard.

A board segment is eight minutes, so the weekly problems never ask you to sustain a calculation across an afternoon. One deep problem does.

  • Before AI, ten minutes, written. Your strategy, the equations you expect to need, what you do not know.
  • Work with AI. No restrictions.
  • Solution. AI may draft it. You verify it and it goes out under your name.
  • Reflection, without AI. Where it was wrong, what you changed, how you knew.

Step 4 is what is assessed. Step 3 is not. Step 1 exists so that step 4 has something to compare against.

The arc

What you check AI against
is withdrawn in stages.

Weeks 2–6

The course lecture notes. A document that is correct, comparable line by line.

Weeks 7–13

Papers. Authoritative in a weaker sense, and they disagree with each other.

The project

Your own map, and nothing else.

What is not withdrawn is the standard in this room. It is the same in week 15 as in week 1.

Weeks 7–9 and 12–13

Ten subjects, ten maps.

Constituents

photons · baryons · neutrinos · dark matter · dark energy · initial conditions

In order of how well we know them, so the sessions open as they go.

Probes

DESI, HETDEX · Roman, Euclid, LSST · LVK, LISA, PTA · SPHEREx

What will sharpen those claims, or break them.

Four segments each: the claim · the measurement · the assumptions · the threat. The board fills into an actual map. One rule: any claim you attribute to a paper, you can point to in that paper.

The project

Find a gap. Make it a question.
Produce the smallest piece of evidence.

End of week 9A research question, approved before I leave
Weeks 10–11Clinic with the TA. Build a prototype
Weeks 12–13Refine it while the class keeps mapping
Weeks 14–15Twenty minutes, and fifteen of questions

The prototype is small by design. Reproduce one published figure. Check one approximation numerically. Analyse a subset of public data. The question is never whether the code runs.

The question the project actually asks

Can you use AI to construct a scientific calculation and establish that its result is trustworthy?

You will have answered this four times by then, against the Friedmann equations, three distance measures, the thermal history, and slow roll. The difference in week 12 is that nobody knows the answer.

Attendance

With eight of us there is no slack. If you are not here, one of the four problems has no presenter and a classmate covers it. Absence is paid for by the other seven before it is paid for by you.

Tell me before the Monday deadline and you come out of the draw, no penalty. Volunteer to cover for someone and you gain a credit.

AI policy, in full

Use it. I encourage it.
Or don't, if you would rather
work it out yourself.

Solutions, derivations, code and literature analysis may be AI-generated in whole or in part. This course is not interested in which route you took. It is interested in whether you can defend where you arrived. Say in your closing line which problems you did which way.

Two exceptions: the opening and closing lines, and the reflection in each problem set. Those are where your judgment rather than your output is visible.

Before Thursday

One problem. Not graded.

Estimate the temperature at which the universe became neutral.

Two numbers are available to you: the binding energy of hydrogen, 13.6 eV, and the ratio of photons to baryons, roughly $10^9$. Use both, and say what each one does.

Then say what you would need in order to do better than an estimate.

Open and close it with the two lines. On Thursday I will work it at the board under the rules you just heard, and you will get to take it apart.
